# ADR-0006: Generators, the noise library and loading

Status: accepted · 12 October 2026

## Context

Phase 4 adds ten generators to the aurora: animated gradients, grain, a `starfield` to sit under it, and the elements: Earth, Air, Fire and Water. They must look beautiful untouched, stay small, and work identically in the worker and on the main thread, including generators apps register themselves.

## Decision

- **A shared WGSL noise library** (`generators/noise.ts`): PCG integer hash (the top 24 bits, so results are exact in f32 and never round up to 1), value, Perlin, simplex, Worley, fbm / ridged / turbulence and curl, every symbol `noise_*`. Generators declare `requires`; the composer includes each module and its dependencies once per shader. Integer hashing instead of `fract(sin(…))` keeps patterns identical across GPUs and free of banding at large coordinates. GPU tests pin ranges, centring, F1 ≤ F2, curl divergence and determinism.
- **Generator specs are data.** A generator is its params, colours, palette, `requires` and WGSL, plus a CSS function. `GeneratorSpec` is everything but the CSS function, so it can be posted. The page sends the worker the spec of each generator a document uses, once per worker. The worker bundles no generator code (8.4 kB), and `registerGenerator()` works with the worker (it silently didn't before).
- **Built-ins load on demand.** `aurora-fbm` ships eagerly; the other ten are separate chunks loaded the first time a document uses them (`loadGenerators()`). `validateDocument()` is async and loads what it checks; `mountAurora` paints the fallback at once, loads, then repaints and starts the GPU path; an unknown generator settles on CSS. Main-thread entry: 7.0 kB.
- **Art direction is part of done.** Every generator has a palette that shows it at its best and defaults tuned on contact sheets of several frames, and a golden image under `packages/core/test/golden`. The Earth generator started as a shaded heightfield (`terrain`), which read as camouflage at background scale; it became `strata`, folded sandstone layers. `water`'s caustics are original (the well-known Shadertoy caustics are CC BY-NC-SA, incompatible with MIT). Air is `clouds`: Perlin-Worley billows (inverted cellular noise gathered by a broad fbm), so clouds are built from round lobes rather than thresholded fbm, which read as streaks; two depths, lit by a short march towards the sun, so bellies shade and tops glow; `wind` stays as Air in motion.
- **Fine detail is band-limited.** Generators know their pixel footprint (`ctx.res`), so periodic detail (strata bedding, caustic lines, embers) fades or widens before it would alias, instead of shimmering on small canvases. This is analytic rather than `fwidth`, so it stays valid in any control flow.
- **Scenes** (`presets/scenes/*.aurora`) are art-directed multi-layer presets with their own golden images, starting with `northern-lights` (an aurora over the starfield) and `golden-hour` (clouds at sunset).
- **Docs and starters are generated** from the specs by a snapshot test (`docs/generators/*.md`, `presets/starters/*.aurora`), so they can't drift. Each page says what every palette colour paints (from `generators/roles.ts`, which the runtime never imports; the test fails if a generator lacks it) and what raising each parameter does, and keeps the noise modules it uses in an implementation note at the end. Each starter is its generator alone. Grain is opt-in: you add it as a layer. Until October 2026 the gradient starters carried a grain overlay, but at the default render scale it read as coarse, flickering noise rather than texture, so it is no longer a default. Grain cells are whole canvas pixels, since a fractional size alternated 1- and 2-pixel cells into a mottled pattern.
- **Budgets**: each generator under 2 kB gzip (largest: starfield, 1.99 kB), the noise library under 3 kB (1.4 kB). WGSL comments ship inside the shader string, so explanations go in the TypeScript doc comment instead.
- **Additions, October 2026.**
  - `linear-gradient`: a plain gradient through two to four palette colours, using CSS's gradient line, so its CSS fallback matches the WebGPU frame to within 1/255. It is the sky behind `mountains` in the `blue-hour` scene.
  - `curtain`: aurora curtains, with a bright, swaying lower edge and vertical rays that climb out of it, up to three folds deep; `tilt` and `arc` sweep and bend the edge across the sky and `glow` fills the space between the rays, modelled on photographs of overhead curtains. `aurora-fbm` stays the bit-exact original and can't draw upright rays (its domain warping smears them), so curtains are a generator of their own.
  - `treeline`: rows of spruce silhouettes on dark ground, with a band of glow along the horizon behind them, for the foreground of night skies. Both reference photographs the hero was matched against have trees on the horizon, and `mountains` reads as rock, not forest. `curtain` also gained `cover`, which fills the gaps along a curtain so it can spread across the whole sky as one band.
  - `rain` and `lightning`, ported from the weather overlay in LoP-Engine: rain falls in three depths and leans with the wind, with streak widths in canvas pixels; lightning strikes in hashed time windows, grows a jagged, branching bolt from the top and double-flickers the sky. Both use the noise library's integer hash instead of LoP's `fract(sin)` hash, so their goldens match on every machine.
  - `starfield` no longer shows rows and diagonals: its three depths used cells of 8, 24 and 64 px, which share grid lines, and kept each star within ±35% of its cell centre. Each depth now has its own turned grid (7, 23 and 59 px) and stars use almost the whole cell.
  - Scenes: `borealis` (a diffuse aurora, green at the horizon rising into magenta and violet rays, over a treeline; the site's hero), `polar-night` (teal and violet aurora over mountains), `northern-lights` (green curtains over a sparse sky, rebuilt on `curtain`) `blue-hour` (mountains against a dusk gradient) and `glacier`, `glacier-night` and `glacier-storm` (one landscape of mountains and a treeline by day, under an aurora at night, and in rain and lightning; the Studio picture under the hero switches between them) `misty-forest` (three rows of spruce fading into a pale morning sky, the treeline's card) and `lagoon` (teal caustics over a drifting mesh gradient) each have their own starfield and palette, so no two look alike.
  - Goldens that include `aurora-fbm` come from CI: its `fract(sin(…))` hash depends on how the CPU's SwiftShader computes `sin`, so an ARM Mac renders it differently from CI's x86. CI uploads `*.actual.png` when a golden fails.

## Consequences

- A generator's first use costs one small request; the CSS fallback covers the gap.
- Custom generators keep working everywhere, as long as they are registered before a surface uses them.
